# Age orientation: only while own data is missing

These values are **starting points with low confidence**. As soon as the log has five good comparison days, the child's observed wake windows from the profile apply. When they disagree, the child always wins, not the table.

## Total sleep per 24 hours (including naps)

Recommendation of the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (Paruthi et al., 2016), endorsed by the American Academy of Pediatrics:

| Age | Total sleep |
|---|---|
| 4–12 months | 12–16 h |
| 1–2 years | 11–14 h |
| 3–5 years | 10–13 h |
| 6–12 years | 9–12 h |

These are ranges for healthy children, not targets for a single day. If a child is persistently well below or above and noticeably affected during the day, talk to the pediatrician.

## Wake windows: rough rules of thumb, not scientifically established

Wake-window figures come from parenting guides and consulting practice, not from studies. They vary a lot between children. Use them only as a wide range and say "rough orientation" in the answer.

| Age (typical sleep structure) | before the nap | after the (last) nap |
|---|---|---|
| 12–15 months (often still 2 naps) | approx. 3–4 h per wake phase | approx. 3½–4½ h |
| 15–24 months (mostly 1 nap) | approx. 4–5½ h | approx. 4½–6 h |
| 2–3 years (1 nap) | approx. 4½–6 h | approx. 5–7 h |
| from approx. 3 years (nap often drops) | without nap: waking to sleep onset approx. 11–13 h | |

## Recognising transitions

- **Two naps to one** (often 12–18 months): the second nap is refused or pushes the night back noticeably.
- **Nap drops** (often 2½–4 years): falling asleep in the evening takes much longer for several days, although the routine fits.

Name transitions only when the log shows them over several days, and discuss them with the parents.

## Under 12 months

No wake-window forecast from this table. Only log; safe sleep, night feeds and growth are topics for the midwife, health visitor and pediatrician.
